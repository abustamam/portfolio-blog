## Where most people start

Most people open a <Term>chat app</Term>, <Term>upload</Term> their résumé file and type something like this:

<pre data-make-it-better><code>{MAKE_IT_BETTER}</code></pre>

That does help a little. The tool tidies the wording, fixes typos and tightens long bullet points. But it doesn't know which job you want, so the result tends to come back generic. It can also add a skill or a number you never had, and that reads as confidently as the true parts.

I keep the upload and the "make it better", and add the job posting plus a few lines about what I want back. Section 1 shows the full version. First, a few things to redact from the file.

## What to redact before you upload

A résumé is mostly about you, and what you share about yourself is your call. Three things should be redacted before you upload the file or paste any of it into the chat app. The easiest way is to save a copy of your résumé, redact the copy and upload that one.

- Your references: their names, phone numbers and emails. They agreed to take a call from an employer, not to be put into an <Term>AI</Term> tool. Delete them from the copy completely.
- Anything from your current employer: client names, internal figures, documents from work. Describe what you did ("kept the client list up to date") without including the list.
- The contact block at the top: your full address, phone and email. The tool doesn't need it to do the work. Your original file still has it for the version you send.

The same goes for anything else you paste. See [what's safe to paste into AI](/ai-guides/never-paste/).

## What to search for

The hard part for me is often knowing what to type into the job board, because the job I'd be good at may go by a title I'd never think of. So I give the tool my experience and ask for job titles to search for, plus the words postings for those titles tend to use. Then I search the job boards myself.

<pre><code>{assemblePrompt(FIND_TITLES)}</code></pre>

## 1. Tailor your résumé to one posting

This is the same upload and the same "make it better", with more around it. The posting goes in so the tool knows what to aim for. The task says to reorder and reword what is already there and add nothing. The last line asks the tool to list anything it added anyway, because that is the part you need to check.

<pre><code>{assemblePrompt(TAILOR_RESUME)}</code></pre>

<p><button type="button" class="ai-link" data-prompt-fill="tailor-resume" data-prompt-target="job-search-builder">Put this in the prompt builder ↓</button></p>

If your AI tool can't take a file, paste the text of your redacted copy under the posting instead.

<p data-paste-not-link>Paste the posting's text, not only its link. Some AI apps can't open links, and some guess what is behind one, so check that the result uses the posting's actual wording.</p>

When the list comes back, check it, then read the new résumé side by side with your original anyway. The list can miss things. Anything you can't back up in an interview comes out.

## 2. A cover letter that doesn't read as AI

A cover letter reads as AI when the tool writes the content. So give it the content: why you want this job, what you bring, and anything you want to explain, in your own rough words. The tool's job is only the order and the structure.

<pre><code>{assemblePrompt(COVER_LETTER)}</code></pre>

<p><button type="button" class="ai-link" data-prompt-fill="cover-letter" data-prompt-target="job-search-builder">Put this in the prompt builder ↓</button></p>

Then cut:

- Any sentence you would not say out loud to the person interviewing you.
- Any claim that is not in your notes.
- Stock openings and closings, and big words you never use.
- Lines that repeat your résumé. The letter is where you say why you want the job.

Read what is left out loud. If it does not sound like you, change the words until it does.

## 3. Practice the interview

One prompt turns the tool into an interviewer. It asks a question, waits for your answer, then asks a follow-up, the way a real interviewer digs into what you said. Answer the way you would say it in the room.

<pre><code>{assemblePrompt(INTERVIEW_PRACTICE)}</code></pre>

<p><button type="button" class="ai-link" data-prompt-fill="interview-practice" data-prompt-target="job-search-builder">Put this in the prompt builder ↓</button></p>

Treat the feedback as a starting point. The tool doesn't know the clinic or the person who will interview you.

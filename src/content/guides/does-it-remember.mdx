## Three things people call memory

You tell a <Term>chat app</Term> something on Monday. On Tuesday you wonder whether it knows. Or you're an hour into one conversation and it gets something wrong that you said at the start. Both come from the same mix-up. "Memory" gets used for three different things, and they work differently.

- **This conversation.** A conversation, which most apps call a chat, is one back-and-forth with the <Term>AI</Term>, from your first message to your last. Everything said in it so far is what the AI works from. Start a new chat and it begins empty.
- **Your past chats.** Your chat history is the list of your earlier chats, kept in your account. It's like a filing cabinet. You can reopen a chat, rename it or delete it.
- **Saved memory.** In some apps, a setting called memory lets the app keep a few facts about you ("I cook for four", "keep answers short") and bring them into new chats. Memory, in this sense, is a feature you can switch on and off.

A new chat starts empty. It can read your saved memory, if the setting is on. It can't read the chat you just left. Whether it can read your past chats depends on the app. Some can, and some can't. The rest of this page is about why, starting with the one that trips people up most: the chat you have open.

## Inside one chat, it rereads everything

The AI doesn't hold a conversation in its head the way a person does. Each time you send a message, the app sends the whole chat so far back to the AI: your messages, its answers, and your new one on the end. The AI reads all of it again from the top, then writes the next reply.

That's how it seems to remember what you said five messages ago. It reads it again every time. Think of a pen pal who forgets each letter as soon as they've replied. To get a sensible answer, you'd have to enclose the whole pile of earlier letters with every new one. That's close to what the app does for you.

## Why a very long chat goes wrong

There's a limit to how much it can reread at once. That limit is called the context window: how much of the conversation the AI can keep in view at once. When a chat grows past it, the earliest parts fall out of view. The AI doesn't say so. It keeps answering, as sure of itself as ever, from whatever it can still see. (I wrote about that in [When AI gets it wrong](/ai-guides/when-ai-is-wrong/).)

Real apps keep far more than eight messages in view, often hundreds of pages' worth, and they tend to blur early details before losing them outright. The picture is scaled down to show the idea.

The same rereading is why long chats use up your <Term>usage limit</Term> faster: every new message makes the app send the whole chat again. [Why long chats use more](/ai-guides/usage-limits/) has the detail.

## Apps that shorten a long chat for you

## New chat or keep going

This is the rule I use.

- **New topic, new chat.** If I've been planning a birthday party and now have a question about a tax form, that's a new chat. The party planning only adds clutter.
- **Same task, same chat.** If I'm still working on the party, I stay. The AI needs what I told it earlier.
- **Confused, start fresh.** If it repeats itself, contradicts something I said, or forgets a rule I gave it, I stop arguing and start a new chat.

Starting fresh loses everything, so bring the important part with you. Before you leave, ask: "Summarize what we've decided so far in five lines. Include anything I told you to avoid." Read it. Fix anything wrong. Check that the rule you care about is in it. Then paste it as the first message of the new chat, followed by your next question.

That's what the "start a new chat with a summary" button does above. The new chat is two lines long, but it has the allergy in it, so the answer is nut-free.

## Your past chats

Most apps keep a list of your earlier chats. Look for it in a side panel. On a phone it's often behind a menu button at the top. Menus differ between apps, so this is the general idea, not exact buttons.

- **Find one.** Scroll the list, or use the search box if the app has one. Searching for a word you remember saves a lot of scrolling.
- **Rename one.** Look for a "more" or three-dot button beside the chat. The menu usually offers rename. A name like "Coast trip, July" beats "New chat" when you come back in a month.
- **Delete one.** The same menu usually has delete. Deleting removes it from your list. What the company keeps is a separate question, covered at the end of this page.
- **Continue one.** Reopen an old chat and it picks up where it left off. It rereads the whole thing, so a very long old chat has the same problem as a long new one.

## Saved memory and personalization

Some apps have a setting that saves facts about you between chats: where you live, that you're vegetarian, that you like short answers. Some save things on their own as you chat. Some save only when you say "remember this." Personalization is the wider name for settings that shape answers to you, like instructions you write once. Not every app has these, and they differ from app to app, so I won't describe a particular one.

To see what yours has stored, open the app's settings and look for a section called memory or personalization. If it's there, it usually shows a list of what it saved. You can read each item and edit or delete it, and there's normally a switch to turn the whole thing off. If you can't find it, the app may not offer it, or it may be tied to a type of account, so check the app's help page. On a work account your employer may have set it already.

It's worth turning on for facts that don't change and that you'd otherwise type again and again. It's the wrong place for anything private, or anything that was true only last week. A saved fact keeps shaping answers until you remove it, so look at the list now and then. If you're not sure you want it, leave it off. You can turn it on later.

## What the company keeps

Your chats sit on the company's computers, not on your phone, and deleting one from your list may not delete every copy right away. Many apps also have a privacy setting about whether your chats can be used to improve the product. Policies differ and change, so read your app's privacy page and set it the way you want. What you control for certain is what goes in, so read [what's safe to paste into AI](/ai-guides/never-paste/) before you paste anything private.

A few people in my community have asked me some version of the same question: *can you just teach us this stuff?*

Not "explain what a large language model is." They've read those articles. They want to sit down with someone who uses these tools every day and get their own work done faster.

So I'm going to run a small, hands-on AI cohort. Before I plan anything, I want to know who's coming and what they need. That's what the [quiz](/ai-quiz) is for.

**[Take the quiz →](/ai-quiz)** It's nine questions and takes about four minutes.

## Who it's for

The people who've asked so far are all over the map. Some run nonprofit and community operations: volunteer schedules, donor lists, board reports, event logistics. Some are in sales and live in spreadsheets. Some already use AI every day and want to go further, into agents, automations, and whether to run models locally or in the cloud. And some just want to stop losing their week to the same three tasks.

That range is the reason I'm not building one generic course. A beginner and someone already running AI daily don't need the same session, and putting them in the same room helps neither of them.

## What you get that you won't get from a course

**Bring one real workflow, leave with it automated.** Your spreadsheet, your weekly report, your inbox. Not a textbook example about a fictional coffee shop. If it doesn't work on your actual task, it doesn't count.

**Taught by a practitioner.** I build software for a living, and I run AI agent pipelines every day. I know where these tools are good, and I know where they confidently make things up, because I've been burned by both. I [wrote about some of that](/writing/six-ways-i-fooled-myself-benchmarking) recently.

**A small local cohort.** In person in Natomas, with an online option for people who can't make it. Small enough that everyone gets their own workflow looked at.

**Built for masjid, school and nonprofit work.** Most AI training assumes you work at a tech company. A lot of the people asking me run community organizations on volunteer time and thin budgets, so that's what the examples come from. We'll also spend real time on privacy and ethics: what you should never paste into an AI tool, and how to get the same help without exposing someone's personal details.

## The three tracks

The quiz puts you in one of three tracks. It measures what you actually do with AI, not how confident you feel about it.

**Foundations.** You're new, or you use AI now and then. We work on the habits that matter most: giving it enough context to get a useful answer, checking what it tells you, and knowing what to keep out of it. You leave with a prompt kit for a task you actually do.

**Applied.** You already get value from AI. Now we point it at your work: cleaning up and analyzing spreadsheets, drafting documents and email you can trust, and turning a recurring task into a workflow you can repeat. You leave with that workflow running on your own files.

**Builder.** You're past the basics and want AI to *do* things. We connect it to email, Sheets and calendars, chain steps into multi-step workflows, and work through the local-vs-cloud trade-offs on cost, privacy and quality. You leave with an automation that runs your task end to end, with a human check where it matters.
